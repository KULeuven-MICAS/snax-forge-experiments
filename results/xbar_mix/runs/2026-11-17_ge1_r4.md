# Run note: GE1 R4, the interconnect's energy by input kind (the xbar_mix points powered), 17 Nov 2026

GE1 (T8) found that a quarter of SNAX-GVSoC's 1.2× gap to the gated cluster is the crossbar's price: every request is priced at `xbar.csv`'s read-write `e_access`, while in the cluster almost every input is a streamer channel that only reads or only writes. X70's xbar_mix points (the interconnect in `strm/xbar_mix.py`'s wrapper, read-only and write-only inputs) were synthesized only (CG5 gated them); this round powers them, so the energy can get X71's factor form (X93, next round). It also does R3's step 6: the dot_3 sweep's energy files into `results/cluster/nangate45/ge/`. No DC ran.

**Result:** 18 points powered (gated, seed 0), all ok. Per access, all read-only saves 25 % to 53 % against all read-write and all write-only −4 % to 47 %; at the cluster's 32 inputs on 32 banks: 2.8406 pJ read-write, 1.8981 read-only (−33.2 %), 1.5010 write-only (−47.2 %). The read-write points sit 0.9 % to 4.8 % above `xbar.csv` (the wrapper's ties change DC's netlist; the stimulus is the same).

- **Read-write against `xbar.csv`:** +0.9 % to +4.8 %, the same stimulus on a different netlist (the wrapper's ties let DC drop logic and restructure: i16_b16 has 36,315 nets here, 37,046 in xbar's, its combinational energy 40.85 against 39.97 pJ a cycle).
- **Read-only** saves at every point, more with more banks per input (0.37 → 0.41 → 0.53 at 16 inputs on 16, 32, 64 banks; 0.33 → 0.49 at 32).
- **Write-only** saves where inputs = banks (0.46, 0.47, 0.29) but less, or not at all, as banks outnumber inputs: 16 inputs on 64 banks costs 3.9 % more than read-write. The combinational energy a cycle is what grows: 21.6, 35.7, 85.3 pJ at 16 inputs on 16, 32, 64 banks (read-only 24.9, 28.2, 35.7). The stimulus writes new random data on every write-only input every cycle (read-write: every other cycle; read-only: never), and the SAIF's toggles on the input-side write data are 2.0× read-write's at both 16 and 64 banks, while those on the bank-side write data are 1.0× read-write's at 16 banks and 1.6× at 64. A likely reading: each input's write data reaches every bank's multiplexer, so a write's data toggles cost with the banks per input. Not shown further here; the factor form (X93, next round) has to decide how to carry it, since the per-pair savings are not one constant.
