# Run note: HCMP1 R4b, the probe's synthesis and power, 13 points, 28 Nov 2026

HCMP1's probe at X118's spec (forge's `i8`: int8 in, int32 products, sums and outputs; W = 8; 4096 elements a stream; data uniform over each type): vecadd, relu and dot in Vitis `opt`, Bambu and XLS (RTL from R4a and R3) and forge (4 new points, dot 3 with and without the narrow multiplier, snax-forge D207), through clock-gated synthesis and power on nangate45 at 10 ns. The power flow now takes narrow element streams and prints one port window for every source (X119).

**Result:** 13 of 13 points synthesize (errors 0, every slack positive) and pass power at n4096: every output beat equals snax-forge's kernel at `i8`, every expected count and BUSY_COUNTER equal the window (`expected_diff` 0, `counter_diff` 0), and `cycles_ports` ≤ `cycles` + 1 everywhere. On the ports every tool takes 512 or 513 cycles for 512 beats, so cycles are comparable. On energy per task forge's control + datapath is within 0.93 to 1.07 × the best HLS point (XLS for all three kernels); with dot's packer dot 3 is 1.11 × and dot 3 narrow 1.04 × (vecadd and relu have no adapter); on area 1.07 to 1.50 ×. The narrow multiplier takes dot 3's multipliers from 2,308.9 to 2,006.7 µm² (−13.1 %) and from 3.626 to 3.238 pJ a cycle (−10.7 %), the point's energy per task from 3,158.1 to 2,958.7 pJ (−6.3 %).

## Step 1: narrow element streams (X119)

- **tb.json.** `sim/sources/hls.py`, `bambu.py` and `xls.py` take each stream's element from `params.json` (an input at `dtype`, an output at `acc_dtype`, else `dtype`; dslx-exercises' for XLS); lanes = TDATA bits / its bits (`lanes_of`); `check` takes axis int8 to int64. The 9 HLS points: inputs 8 int8 lanes in 64 bits, vecadd's and relu's outputs 8 int32 lanes in 256 bits, dot's `out` one int32.
- **Stimulus and reference.** `pwr.py` draws each input uniform over its stream's dtype from seed 0 (int64: the same `rng.integers` call as X19, so the same draws); `beats_hex` packs lanes at their width; `axis_sink.sv` gets a lane width `EW` (written into `tb.sv` only when not 64) for its mismatch print. A narrow point's `tb.json` carries `ref` (snax-forge's kernel, its preset `i8` and the dtypes snax-forge resolves, from the new `forge/query.py types`, each stream checked against its container); the expected outputs are `forge/query.py ref` on the inputs at those dtypes (`kernel_ref`, as forge's check), once per kernel and inputs. `sim/ref.py` stays for int64.
- **Sizes.** A narrow point has its `params.json` size and `b64` (`n4096`, `b64`); no `n2048`. Bambu's and XLS's expected cycles take the cycle CSV's row at that size (the line needs two sizes; the int64 points get the same value, the line being exact at its sizes).
- **The int64 check.** `pwr tbjson` before and after the change on one int64 point of each source, plus two more: `hls done/vecmul/opt_w8`, `hls untiered/rowsum/opt_w8`, `bambu done/vecmul/bambu_w8`, `bambu untiered/axpy/bambu_w8`, `xls done/vecmul/xls_w8`, `xls untiered/axpy/xls_w8`: byte for byte identical. forge's `done/vecmul/forge_w8`: its committed bundle names a design point no longer in snax-forge's `out/`, and its DC netlist predates the `_v1_` top rename (PITFALLS E10), so it was rebundled into a scratch folder with the unchanged `bundles.py` (snax-forge `5c5d0ed7`) and its `tb.json` built from a port list of the wrapper before and after the change: identical. The committed bundle and netlist were not touched.

## Step 2: one port window

Every sequencer (`ap_ctrl_hs_seq`, `bambu_seq`, `xls_seq`, `csr_seq`) takes `in_hs` and `out_hs` from the generated top (valid and ready on any input stream, any output stream; XLS's cfg is not a stream), counts rising edges from reset's release (the first edge after it is cycle 1) and prints `TB: ports first_in <c> last_out <c>`; `pwr.py` writes `cycles_ports` = last_out − first_in + 1 beside `cycles` (a run without the line fails). The energy window stays each source's. Per point below: Vitis and Bambu 514 / 513 (`cycles` / `cycles_ports`), XLS 513 / 512, forge vecadd and relu 512 / 512, dot 513 / 513: `cycles_ports` ≤ `cycles` + 1 for every source. `power_pt.csv` of each source gains the column (empty on older rows); `power_pt_modules.csv` keeps n4096 runs' modules (`MODULE_SIZES`).
