# Run note: HLS opt at a 10 ns HLS clock, vecadd and vecmul W = 8, 1 Nov 2026

A first, short try at the question of the analysis note's section 10: does Vitis drop the register stage between the stream read and the stream write when its clock target is the ASIC's 10 ns instead of the default 3.33 ns? One Vitis run per kernel, then the Verilog read; nothing synthesized or simulated beyond Vitis's own csim and cosim.

**No: the register stage stays.** vecadd's Verilog is identical to the 3.33 ns point's apart from the `CORE_GENERATION_INFO` line (its clock fields). vecmul's moves its registers from the operands to the products, half the bits. Both keep a pipeline of depth 2 and cosim's 10 cycles for 8 beats, as at 3.33 ns.

The host's `python3` (3.6) cannot run bench.py; `python3.11` (standard library only) can.
- Every other setting as the 3.33 ns points: opt directives (`BEAT` pipelined at II = 1, `LANE` unrolled), axis ports with `register_mode off`, the `loop_count` pin as `ap_vld`.

## Results

| point | csim, cosim | cosim cycles (8 beats) | pipeline depth | datapath registers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vecadd/opt_w8` (3.33 ns) | pass | 10 | 2 | results `add_ln13_*_reg`, 8 × 64 = 512 bits, driving `C_TDATA` |
| `vecadd/opt_w8_c10` | pass | 10 | 2 | the same: Verilog identical apart from `CORE_GENERATION_INFO` |
| `vecmul/opt_w8` (3.33 ns) | pass | 10 | 2 | operands `temp_*_reg`, 16 × 64 = 1024 bits; the multipliers drive `C_TDATA` directly |
| `vecmul/opt_w8_c10` | pass | 10 | 2 | products `mul_ln13_*_reg`, 8 × 64 = 512 bits, driving `C_TDATA`; the operands are not registered |

- Vitis's own timing estimate (`HLS_SYN_CLOCK`): vecadd 2.101 ns at both targets; vecmul 3.648 ns at 3.33 ns, 4.664 ns at 10 ns (the multiply now chained after the read).
- The pins, loop counter, FSM and pipeline enables are the same at both targets (pins 33, loop state 32, control 5 bits, by the parser of `report/analysis_syn1.py`).

## Reading

- The stage is not timing-driven: at 10 ns there is room to chain read, compute and write, and Vitis still places the write one stage after the read. It moves the stage where the clock lets it (vecmul: after the multiply), and keeps it.
- For vecmul the 10 ns point registers half the bits, so its datapath flops' internal energy would fall by about half; vecadd's would not change. Not measured: no synthesis or power run was made on these points.
- Next to try, if wanted: a latency constraint on the beat loop (`set_directive_latency -max 0 <top>/BEAT`), or a combinational top, to see whether Vitis can remove the stage at all.
