# Run note: RAM1 R2, the SRAM lookup table, 6 Nov 2026

CACTI 7 run on every depth from 32 to 4096 in steps of 32 words and every width from 8 to 256 in steps of 8 bits: 4,096 points, 4,095 modelled (32 x 8 is under 64 bytes). They are committed as a lookup table, `results/ram/nangate45/ram_table.csv` (X48), which replaces R1's `ram_cacti.csv`. Tables of other densities and curve fits were scored against all 4,095 points before choosing; no fit is kept.

**Run in Claude's sandbox, not on the MICAS server.** All 25 of R1's grid points (server, g++ 8.5.0) come out identical to the last digit in this build (g++ 13.3.0), in every column compared (area, both energies, access and cycle time, leakage, Nspd).

## The table

`pixi run ram table -j N` (`ram.py`'s new `table` command; R1's `all`, its grid, check points and variants are removed, and so is `ram_cacti.csv`, whose numbers stay in R1's note). It runs `vdd_check` first (24 itrs-hp lines, all 1.0 V), then every point, then writes the rows CACTI models, sorted by depth then width. From scratch it took 255 s on 2 cores (about 0.12 s per point per core; R1 measured 0.2 s on the server); cached, 6 s. A cold rerun of 512 x 64, 128 x 256, 4032 x 120 and 480 x 200 in a new folder gave the same numbers.

Columns: `depth`, `width`, `area_um2`, `e_rd_pj`, `e_wr_pj` (1.1 V, x 1.21 from CACTI's 1.0 V), `t_access_ns`, `t_cycle_ns`, `leak_uw`, `ndwl`, `ndbl`, `nspd`, `ndcm`, `ndsam_l1`, `ndsam_l2`. 4,096 lines, 289 KB.

| quantity | smallest | largest |
|---|---|---|
| area | 323 µm² (32 x 16) | 368,338 µm² (4096 x 256) |
| read energy | 0.192 pJ (64 x 8) | 73.6 pJ (4096 x 256) |
| write energy | 0.297 pJ (64 x 8) | 98.8 pJ (4096 x 256) |
| access time | 0.143 ns | 0.702 ns |
| cycle time | 0.097 ns | 0.935 ns |

The banks the cluster uses, looked up:

| bank | area µm² | read pJ | write pJ | read fJ/bit | write fJ/bit | access ns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCDM, 512 x 64 | 13,523 | 3.538 | 6.468 | 55.3 | 101.1 | 0.269 |
| icache data, 128 x 256 | 19,246 | 9.551 | 16.678 | 37.3 | 65.1 | 0.235 |
| icache tag, 128 x 40 (39 padded) | 2,287 | 1.101 | 2.215 | 27.5 | 55.4 | 0.198 |

DaCe's dot buffer is 512 x 64, the TCDM bank's row.

Two of R1's statements hold only on R1's small grid. Over the full range Ndwl is always 2, but Ndbl is 2 or 4 and Nspd 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 (358, 865, 2,077, 670 and 125 points). Write over read runs from 0.65 to 3.36, and write is below read on 795 points, not only at 4096 x 64: exactly the points with Nspd 8 or 16 (median 3104 x 40, deep and narrow), and none other.

## Tables against fits

The reference is the 4,095 points. Each candidate is scored by its relative error at every point: the mean, the 95th percentile, the worst point, and the worst of the banks we build (512, 1024, 2048 and 4096 x 64, 256 x 64, 128 x 256, 128 x 40). Tables interpolate between their own points, bilinearly in log₂ d and log₂ w (or linearly in d and w where marked). The points a table needs that the reference lacks (depth 48) were run as well. The scoring script was a scratch probe and is not committed.

At a similar number of points, log spacing is as good on average as fixed steps and exact at the power-of-two banks, which fixed steps miss at small depths (128, 64). Denser tables lower the mean, but the worst point stays near 18 / 30 / 42 % at any density.

### Fits

All five forms fitted on all 4,095 points; polynomials of total degree 2 or 3; fits in log space predict ln y, the others are least squares on relative error.

| fit | area mean / max | read mean / max | write mean / max | our banks, max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| power law, ln y = c₀ + a ln d + b ln w | 3.0 / 55.7 % | 9.8 / 66.4 % | 9.0 / 38.7 % | 28.2 % |
| second order in d, w | 2.3 / 15.8 % | 6.6 / 25.9 % | 12.9 / 127.2 % | 45.5 % |
| third order in d, w | 1.5 / 17.7 % | 6.2 / 23.3 % | 8.9 / 50.0 % | 21.3 % |
| second order in log₂ d, log₂ w | 2.2 / 34.1 % | 6.7 / 38.4 % | 6.8 / 27.3 % | 13.6 % |
| third order in log₂ d, log₂ w | 1.6 / 21.8 % | 6.1 / 22.0 % | 6.5 / 29.9 % | 12.5 % |

Fitted on the powers-of-2 or half-octave points instead, the same forms score within about 5 points of these on the mean. Fitted on the small grid only (depths 32 to 256, widths 8 to 128) and extrapolated, the power law misses our banks by up to 62 %, second order in d, w by up to 330 %, third order in log₂ d, log₂ w by up to 570 % and third order in d, w by a factor of nearly 400.

### Why no table or fit beats a few percent at worst

CACTI's own numbers are not smooth. Against the mean of its four neighbours on the 32 x 8 grid, a point differs by 0.4 / 1.1 / 1.0 % on average (area / read / write), 1.9 / 8.0 / 7.3 % at the 95th percentile, and up to 15 / 28 / 30 %, where the organization changes between neighbours:

| point | values (area µm², read pJ, write pJ) | neighbours in depth (−32, +32) | Nspd (−32, point, +32) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4032 x 120 | 209,108; 30.91; 28.23 | 170,713 / 29.68 / 41.20; 210,469 / 31.02 / 28.32 | 4, 8, 8 |
| 480 x 200 | 42,329; 14.07; 22.63 | 36,656 / 9.53 / 29.77; 41,236 / 10.29 / 33.46 | 1, 2, 1 |
| 224 x 24 | 2,331; 0.872; 2.068 | 2,032 / 0.82 / 1.84; 2,600 / 1.10 / 1.63 | 1, 1, 2 |

Along the TCDM's width the steps are mild: at 64 bits, depths 448 to 576 stay at Nspd 2, with read 3.44 to 3.73 pJ and write 5.99 to 7.04 pJ.

## Choice

The table is the full 4,095-point grid (Ryan, 6 Nov), used as a lookup table: exact for every bank whose depth is a multiple of 32 words and whose width is a multiple of 8 bits, up to 4096 x 256. No curve fit is kept: the best one is still 6 % off on average for energy and up to 30 % at its worst point, and it is less flexible than the table. Sizes off the grid are left to HWCOST (snax-forge). PITFALLS C5 records why interpolation is not advised.
