### Why the banks are 6.1 times D184's: the copy's bank accesses

PT gives every one of the 32 banks the same internal power in a window, to seven digits (71.18 µW in the run window, 35.90 µW in int8's dma_in, 56.05 µW in its dma_out), whatever its traffic, though int64's column reads (pitch 512) all fall in one bank. Counting each bank's `req_i[0]` and `we_i[0]` high time in the run window's SAIF (`pwr/2_run/w.saif`, inside `sim/runs/snb/nangate45/<run>.zip`, `gen_banks[i].i_data_mem`):

| Run | per bank `req_i` high | per bank `we_i` high | banks | reads | writes | per burst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| int8 | 2 048 cycles | 1 024 cycles | 32, all equal | 32 768 | 32 768 | 8 + 8 |
| int64 | 2 048 cycles | 1 024 cycles | 32, all equal | 32 768 | 32 768 | 8 + 8 |

Each one-element burst reads and writes a whole superbank (8 banks of 64 bits, the DMA's 512-bit beat), the element's byte enables only narrowing the write; D184 and SNAX-GVSoC (R7d) count one bank read and one bank write. With the macro's `when` energies and independent probabilities (PITFALLS PT7), a bank's energy per cycle is P(req) [(1 − P(we)) 3.538 + P(we) 6.468] = 0.1867 × 3.812 = 0.7117 pJ, PT's 71.18 µW at 10 ns; the counts being the same in every bank, so is the energy (PITFALLS PT11). Counted, the banks are 32 768 × (3.538 + 6.468) pJ = 327.87 nJ, exactly 8 × D184's 40.98 nJ; PT's averaged 251.0 nJ is 23.4 % under the count, PT7's bias at these counts (12.5 % on the probe). This contradicts X95's reading of the banks' part as D184's accesses plus what D184 leaves out, so it is X96; X95 is unchanged (it has no results line).

## What it means for D184 as a lower bound

D184's price, 54.48 nJ, is 5.4 % (int8) and 5.2 % (int64) of the copy's dynamic energy on the gate-level cluster: a lower bound by a factor of about 19. Of the 95 % it misses:

- The banks: 251 nJ measured (24 % of the window) against 41 nJ priced. The largest part of this gap is not left-out hardware but the access count: the RTL touches 8 banks each way per burst (X96). At the RTL's 32 768 reads and writes, D184's own table energies give 327.9 nJ; PT's averaged figure is below that (PT7).
- The crossbar: 366 to 382 nJ for `dma` + `tcdm_interconnect` against 13.5 nJ priced. Almost all of it is the `dma` group (349 to 365 nJ, the iDMA engine with its AXI-to-memory and the wide path), which D167 leaves out; the narrow TCDM interconnect, the part closest to D184's crossbar, is 17.4 nJ, 1.3 times D184's 13.49 nJ.
- Not priced at all: 399 to 406 nJ (39 % of the window), mostly the wide AXI crossbar and converters (191 to 205 nJ), the cluster peripherals (81 nJ, constant per cycle), the TCDM bank logic (95 to 101 nJ) and the core waiting on the DMA (17 nJ).

The element's size barely matters: int64 costs 2.3 % more than int8 for 8 times the bytes, all in the DMA's path; the banks are the same. A per-burst price fits the RTL better than a per-byte one, but its bank term is 8 accesses each way, not 1.
